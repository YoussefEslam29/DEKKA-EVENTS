# Tone of Voice

## The brand in one line

Warm, communal, a little artsy/indie — it's a live-music cafe, not a corporate event
platform. Copy should read like **an invitation**, not a listing.

> "Join us Thursday night" — not "Event Registration Available"

## Where this comes from

Dekka (دكة) means "bench" or "seating platform" — the low communal bench you'd share
with friends at a cafe. That's baked into the copy itself: the sign-up hero line is
literally **"Take your place on the bench"** (خُد مكانك على الدكة), turning the brand
name into an invitation rather than treating it as just a logo.

## Real examples from the app

These are shipped copy, not placeholders — use them as the reference for anything new.

| Context | English | Arabic |
|---|---|---|
| Login hero | "Where heritage meets the brew." | "حيث يلتقي التراث بالقهوة." |
| Login sub | "Live music nights in the heart of Dekka." | "ليالي موسيقى حيّة في قلب دكة." |
| Sign-up hero | "Take your place on the bench." | "خُد مكانك على الدكة." |
| Sign-up sub | "Reserve, sip, and hear the music with us." | "احجز، اشرب، واسمع الموسيقى معانا." |
| Welcome back | "Welcome Back" | "أهلاً بعودتك" |
| Welcome back sub | "Sign in to hold your spot at the next night." | "سجّل دخولك عشان تحجز مكانك في الحفلة الجاية." |
| Tagline | "Coffee & live music" | "قهوة وموسيقى حيّة" |

Notice the pattern: short, warm, sensory verbs (*sip*, *hear*, *hold your spot*) instead
of transactional ones (*register*, *submit*, *purchase*). Arabic copy leans slightly
informal/colloquial in places ("معانا" — "with us", conversational) rather than
stiffly formal MSA — it should sound like the cafe owner talking to a regular, not a
government form.

## Arabic-first, not Arabic-translated

Arabic is the **default** language (RTL), and English is the toggle — not the other
way around. When writing new copy, draft the Arabic first as the primary voice, then
write an English line that carries the same warmth rather than a literal translation.
A flat, literal translation of a warm Arabic line usually reads stiff in English —
adjust the English phrasing to match the *feeling*, not the exact words (see how
"خُد مكانك على الدكة" — literally "take your seat on the bench" — became "Take your
place on the bench," not "Take your seat on the Dekka").

## Where to be warm vs. where to be plain

- **Warm/invitational:** hero headlines, empty states, confirmation messages, the
  "About" page, anything a guest reads.
- **Plain and clear:** transactional copy — form labels, error messages, admin/staff
  tool copy. A staff member checking someone in at the door needs "Name", "Phone",
  "Payment Method" — not a clever line. Save the voice for guest-facing moments.

## Bilingual label mechanics

See `02-typography.md` for the `English Label / العربية` pattern used on every shared
field label, button, and heading. That's a formatting rule, not a tone rule — the tone
guidance above applies to the *words chosen*, the bilingual pattern applies to *how
they're laid out*.
